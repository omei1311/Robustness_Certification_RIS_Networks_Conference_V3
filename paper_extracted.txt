

Configuration-Specific Robustness Certification for Stability-Aware Selection of Discrete RIS Configurations
Abstract
This paper studies configuration-specific robustness certification for discrete reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted multi-cell wireless networks under bounded channel uncertainty. Conventional robust RIS optimization establishes feasibility inside a prescribed uncertainty set, but does not directly quantify the remaining robustness of an obtained configuration. We define a configuration-specific robustness certificate as the largest uncertainty radius for which a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration remains robustly feasible, and compute it using repeated statewise robust QoS feasibility checks with bisection. The certificate induces a robustness profile for each candidate configuration and enables dominance filtering and stability-aware selection in the wireless energy-efficiency (WEE) versus robustness plane. Under a bounded channel-drift model, the certificate can further be interpreted as a conservative certified reuse horizon. The resulting framework therefore extends static robust RIS optimization with a configuration-level certification and selection layer rather than another full discrete optimization procedure.
1. Introduction
RIS provides a programmable means of reshaping wireless propagation, but practical systems face finite phase resolution, hardware/controller power consumption, and imperfect channel state information (CSI). Robust optimization protects QoS against bounded CSI errors, yet the uncertainty radius is normally prescribed before optimization. Consequently, the resulting solution establishes feasibility inside that radius but does not directly quantify the residual robustness of the obtained configuration.
This paper focuses on the complementary problem of configuration-specific certification. For a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration X, we define its robustness certificate as the largest uncertainty radius under which all robust QoS constraints remain satisfied. The certificate is obtained without re-optimizing X, making it suitable for evaluating multiple already-generated candidate configurations from an existing robust design procedure. Rather than treating the certificate as a single scalar only, we use the nested feasible-radius property to define a monotone robustness profile and to compare candidate configurations jointly in the WEE-robustness plane.
We further use the certificate for configuration management. When several QoS-feasible configurations are available, a configuration with the highest current WEE need not have the largest robustness margin. We therefore formulate a dominance-based filtering rule and a robustness-constrained WEE selection problem. If a channel-drift model is available, the same certificate can be mapped to a conservative reuse horizon; this temporal interpretation is treated as a corollary of the robustness certificate rather than as a separate contribution.
The contributions are: (1) a configuration-specific robustness certificate and associated monotone robustness profile derived from the statewise robust QoS feasibility condition; (2) a dominance/Pareto filtering principle for removing configurations that are simultaneously inferior in WEE and certified robustness; and (3) a stability-aware configuration-selection rule that maximizes WEE subject to a minimum robustness requirement, with certified reuse time presented as a conditional interpretation under an explicit channel-drift model.
2. System Model and Robust WEE Formulation
Consider a multi-cell RIS-assisted downlink system with L cells and K users per cell. Let W denote the transmit beamforming variables and Θ the discrete RIS configuration. For B-bit phase resolution,
θ_n ∈ 𝒬_B = {exp(j 2πq/2^B) : q=0,1,…,2^B−1},  n=1,…,N.   (1)
For a fixed configuration X=(W,Θ), the SINR of user k in cell l is
γ_lk(X,h) = |h_lk^H w_lk|² / (Σ_(i,j)≠(l,k) |h_lk^H w_ij|² + σ²).   (2)
The imperfect channel is modeled as
h_lk = ĥ_lk + Δh_lk,    ||Δh_lk||₂ ≤ r.   (3)
For target SINR γ̄_lk, robust QoS requires
γ_lk(X, ĥ_lk+Δh_lk) ≥ γ̄_lk,  for all ||Δh_lk||₂≤r.   (4)
The wireless energy efficiency is
WEE(X) = [Σ_l,k R_lk(X)] / P_tot(X),   R_lk=B_w log₂(1+γ_lk).   (5)
where P_tot includes transmit, circuit, and RIS hardware/controller power.
The robust WEE design problem is
max_{W,Θ} WEE(W,Θ)
s.t. γ_lk(W,Θ,Δh) ≥ γ̄_lk,
     ∀l,k, ∀Δh: ||Δh||₂≤ε,
     W∈𝒲, Θ∈𝒬_B^N.   (6)
The present paper treats X as fixed during certification. Candidate configurations can be generated by the robust WEE optimizer of the preceding design stage or by any other feasible configuration-generation method. The certification layer therefore does not claim another global optimization algorithm; its purpose is to quantify and compare the robustness of obtained configurations.
3. Statewise Robust QoS Reformulation
For fixed X, each robust QoS constraint can be written as a quadratic inequality
(ĥ+Δh)^H A_lk(X)(ĥ+Δh) − γ̄_lk σ² ≥ 0.   (7)
The uncertainty set is
Δh^H I Δh − r² ≤ 0.   (8)
By the S-procedure, the robust QoS condition is equivalent, under the standard regularity condition, to the existence of λ_lk≥0 such that
M_lk(X,r,λ_lk) =
[ A_lk+λ_lk I,              A_lk ĥ_lk
  ĥ_lk^H A_lk,  ĥ_lk^H A_lk ĥ_lk−γ̄_lk σ²−λ_lk r² ] ⪰ 0.   (9)
Thus, for fixed X and r, robust feasibility can be checked by finding nonnegative multipliers satisfying (9) for every user. This statewise feasibility test is the oracle used by the proposed certificate computation. The equivalence concerns fixed-state robust QoS feasibility and does not imply global optimality of the overall mixed discrete-continuous WEE problem.
4. Configuration-Specific Robustness Certificate
For a fixed configuration X that is nominally feasible, define the robustness-feasibility indicator
F_X(r) = 1 if X satisfies all robust QoS constraints for uncertainty radius r, and F_X(r) = 0 otherwise.   (10)
R_cert(X) = sup { r ≥ 0 : F_X(r)=1 }.   (11)
R_cert(X) is the maximum uncertainty radius that the already-fixed configuration can tolerate while preserving robust QoS under the adopted uncertainty model. The function F_X(r) is monotone non-increasing in r because norm-bounded uncertainty sets are nested: if X is feasible at r2, it is also feasible at every 0 ≤ r1 ≤ r2. Hence R_cert is the boundary between the certified and uncertified radius regions.
Because F_X(r) is monotone, the certificate can be obtained by bisection. With bounds r_low and r_high,
 r_mid=(r_low+r_high)/2.   (12)
If F_X(r_mid)=1, set r_low=r_mid; otherwise set r_high=r_mid. Stop when
 r_high−r_low ≤ δ_r.   (13)
The returned r_low is a conservative numerical certificate. No beamforming or RIS re-optimization is required during this stage. More importantly, R_cert allows candidate configurations to be compared on a common robustness axis even when they have different current WEE values.
5. Certified Reuse Horizon (Corollary)
Let the reference channel be ĥ(0) and let the channel deviation be bounded by a general nondecreasing function ρ(t):
 ||ĥ(t)−ĥ(0)||₂ ≤ ρ(t).   (14)
A fixed configuration X is certified as reusable at time t whenever
 ρ(t) ≤ R_cert(X).
Thus define the certified reuse horizon as
T_cert(X)=sup{t ≥ 0 : ρ(t) ≤ R_cert(X)}.   (15)
This definition does not require linear channel evolution. As a special case, if the adopted deterministic drift model is ρ(t)=νt with ν>0, then
 T_cert(X)=R_cert(X)/ν.   (16)
Hence the division by ν is a direct consequence of a particular drift model, not an independent optimization result. T_cert is a conservative certificate under the assumed drift model, not a prediction of the actual QoS failure time.
6. Robustness-Aware Configuration Selection and Dominance Filtering
Given a candidate set 𝒳={X₁,…,X_J}, a WEE-only rule selects
 X_WEE = arg max_{X∈𝒳} WEE(X).   (17)
However, a candidate with lower WEE can have a substantially larger robustness certificate. We therefore consider the two-dimensional representation (WEE(X), R_cert(X)). A configuration Xi dominates Xj if
 WEE(Xi) ≥ WEE(Xj)  and  R_cert(Xi) ≥ R_cert(Xj),
with at least one strict inequality. Dominated configurations can be removed before any threshold-based selection because they cannot be preferred by a policy that is nondecreasing in both WEE and certified robustness. The remaining nondominated set forms the WEE-robustness Pareto frontier.
To account for configuration stability, we then impose a minimum robustness requirement R_min and solve
 X_sel = arg max_{X∈𝒳} WEE(X)
 s.t. R_cert(X) ≥ R_min.   (18)
Under the linear drift model in Section 5, the same rule may equivalently be expressed using a minimum certified reuse horizon,
 R_cert(X) ≥ νT_min,   equivalently   T_cert(X) ≥ T_min.   (19)
Thus, configurations that cannot satisfy the required robustness or reuse duration are first filtered out; among the remaining nondominated or feasible candidates, the configuration with the highest WEE is selected. The robustness-only rule X_R=arg max R_cert(X) can be reported as a reference, but it is not the proposed objective.
7. Proposed Certification and Selection Framework
The framework contains five stages: (i) generate a pool of QoS-feasible candidate beamforming/RIS configurations; (ii) certify each fixed configuration by bisection using the statewise LMI feasibility test; (iii) construct the WEE-robustness candidate map and remove dominated configurations; (iv) apply a minimum-robustness requirement and select the highest-WEE remaining candidate; and (v) when a drift model is available, map the selected certificate to a conservative reuse horizon.
The complete logic is
 Robust Configuration Design → Candidate Pool → Statewise Robust Feasibility → R_cert → Dominance/Pareto Filtering → Stability-Aware Selection → [T_cert].   (20)
The certification layer is modular and can be applied to any candidate for which the statewise robust QoS feasibility test is available. Candidate generation is decoupled from certification, while the selection layer uses only candidate-level WEE and certificate values. This separation allows the proposed framework to be attached to different robust RIS optimizers without changing the underlying candidate-generation algorithm.
8. Complexity Analysis
Let C_LMI be the cost of one statewise feasibility check and I_bis the number of bisection iterations. Certification of one configuration costs approximately
O(I_bis C_LMI).   (21)
For initial interval [r_low,r_high] and tolerance δ_r,
I_bis = O(log₂((r_high−r_low)/δ_r)).   (22)
For J candidates, the certification overhead is
O(J I_bis C_LMI).   (23)
Certification does not introduce a new search over the discrete RIS space and does not repeat the full WEE optimization.
9. Numerical Evaluation
The numerical study is organized into three main experiments so that the core claims can be demonstrated within a conference-paper page budget. The same candidate-generation protocol and system parameters are used throughout, and only configurations passing the prescribed QoS feasibility check enter the certification stage.
Experiment 1 — Certificate validation. Select several representative feasible configurations and compute R_cert by bisection. For each configuration, sweep the realized channel-deviation radius over a range covering approximately 0–1.5R_cert and evaluate the minimum user SINR over independent uncertainty samples. Plot the QoS level together with the target threshold and mark R_cert. A second panel can report the empirical violation rate versus radius. The Monte Carlo sweep is only an empirical validation of the deterministic LMI certificate; it does not replace the certificate calculation.
Experiment 2 — Efficiency–robustness landscape and Pareto structure. Build a candidate pool of roughly 20–50 unique feasible configurations from multiple robust-optimization starts or channel realizations. For every candidate, report WEE and R_cert and visualize the joint distribution. Highlight the WEE-only choice, the robustness-only choice, the nondominated Pareto set, and representative dominated points. This experiment tests whether current efficiency and certified robustness contain complementary configuration information. If the points are highly aligned, the candidate-generation protocol should be expanded to obtain a sufficiently diverse feasible pool rather than forcing an artificial tradeoff.
Experiment 3 — Stability-aware selection and requirement sensitivity. Compare three selection rules: WEE-only, robustness-only, and the proposed robustness-constrained WEE rule. Then vary the minimum robustness requirement R_min over a normalized grid, e.g., {0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90}R_max, where R_max is the largest certificate in the candidate pool. Report selected WEE, R_cert, T_cert, and the number of candidates remaining after filtering. This experiment demonstrates whether the certificate changes the selected configuration and how stricter stability requirements move the operating point. Because T_cert follows from the stated drift model, a separate main-paper sweep over ν is unnecessary; one representative drift bound is sufficient.
Across all experiments, the prescribed design uncertainty radius ε and the post-optimization certificate R_cert must be reported separately. R_cert measures a property of a fixed configuration under the adopted uncertainty model, whereas T_cert additionally depends on the channel-drift assumption. If space permits, independent channel realizations can be used as a compact robustness check in Experiment 3 rather than as a separate experiment.
10. Discussion
The proposed certificate provides configuration-level information that a conventional robust design result does not directly report. Two feasible configurations with similar WEE can have different certified uncertainty margins. The WEE-robustness map then reveals whether one configuration dominates another and identifies the nondominated set that contains the meaningful candidates for stability-aware selection.
The interpretation is model-dependent. R_cert is defined with respect to the adopted uncertainty set and QoS constraints. T_cert additionally depends on the assumed drift-rate or channel-aging model and is therefore a derived temporal interpretation rather than a standalone robustness metric. Statistical channel-aging models or empirically calibrated drift bounds can lead to different lifetime interpretations. The reported lifetime should be described as a certified reuse horizon under explicit assumptions, not as the actual QoS failure time.
The framework also does not claim global optimality for the underlying discrete RIS WEE optimization. Its contribution is a configuration-level certification, dominance filtering, and selection layer after candidate configurations have been obtained. The practical benefit therefore depends on the quality and diversity of the candidate pool; certification does not create new candidates by itself.
11. Conclusion
This paper proposed a configuration-specific robustness certification framework for discrete RIS-assisted multi-cell networks. For a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration, the framework determines the maximum bounded CSI uncertainty radius preserving robust QoS through a statewise S-procedure-based feasibility test and bisection. The resulting certificate defines a configuration-level robustness axis on which feasible candidates can be dominance-filtered and selected jointly with WEE. A reuse horizon is obtained only as a conditional corollary under an explicit channel-drift model. The framework therefore extends static robust RIS optimization with a reusable certification and configuration-management layer, without introducing another full discrete optimization procedure.
Key notation: X=(W,Θ) denotes a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration; F_X(r) is its robust-feasibility indicator; R_cert is its configuration-specific robustness certificate; T_cert is the conditional certified reuse horizon; R_min is the minimum robustness requirement; ε is the prescribed design uncertainty radius; ν is the adopted linear channel-drift-rate bound.
