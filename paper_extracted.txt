

Configuration-Specific Robustness Certification for Stability-Aware Selection of Discrete RIS Configurations
Abstract
This paper studies configuration-specific robustness certification for discrete reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted multi-cell wireless networks under bounded channel uncertainty. Conventional robust RIS optimization establishes feasibility inside a prescribed uncertainty set, but does not directly quantify the remaining robustness of an obtained configuration. We define a configuration-specific robustness certificate as the largest dimensionless relative uncertainty factor for which a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration remains robustly feasible, and compute it using repeated statewise robust QoS feasibility checks with bisection. The certificate induces a monotone robustness profile for each candidate configuration and enables dominance filtering and stability-aware selection in the wireless energy-efficiency (WEE) versus robustness plane. Under a bounded relative channel-drift model, the certificate can further be interpreted as a conservative certified reuse horizon. The resulting framework therefore extends static robust RIS optimization with a configuration-level certification and selection layer rather than another full discrete optimization procedure.
1. Introduction
RIS provides a programmable means of reshaping wireless propagation, but practical systems face finite phase resolution, hardware/controller power consumption, and imperfect channel state information (CSI). Robust optimization protects QoS against bounded CSI errors, yet the uncertainty level is normally prescribed before optimization. Consequently, the resulting solution establishes feasibility inside that prescribed level but does not directly quantify the residual robustness of the obtained configuration.
This paper focuses on the complementary problem of configuration-specific certification. For a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration X, we define its robustness certificate as the largest dimensionless relative uncertainty factor under which all robust QoS constraints remain satisfied. The certificate is obtained without re-optimizing X, making it suitable for evaluating multiple already-generated candidate configurations from an existing robust design procedure. Rather than treating the certificate as a single scalar only, we use the nested feasible-factor property to define a monotone robustness profile and to compare candidate configurations jointly in the WEE-robustness plane.
We further use the certificate for configuration management. When several QoS-feasible configurations are available, a configuration with the highest current WEE need not have the largest certified robustness reserve. We therefore formulate a dominance-based filtering rule and a robustness-constrained WEE selection problem. If a relative channel-drift model is available, the same certificate can be mapped to a conservative reuse horizon; this temporal interpretation is treated as a corollary of the robustness certificate rather than as a separate contribution.
The contributions are: (1) a configuration-specific robustness certificate, expressed as a relative uncertainty scaling factor, and its associated monotone robustness profile derived from the statewise robust QoS feasibility condition; (2) a dominance/Pareto filtering principle for removing configurations that are simultaneously inferior in WEE and certified robustness; and (3) a stability-aware configuration-selection rule that maximizes WEE subject to a minimum robustness requirement, with certified reuse time presented as a conditional interpretation under an explicit relative channel-drift model.
2. System Model and Robust WEE Formulation
Consider a multi-cell RIS-assisted downlink system with L cells and K users per cell. Let W denote the transmit beamforming variables and Θ the discrete RIS configuration. For B-bit phase resolution,
θ_n ∈ 𝒬_B = {exp(j 2πq/2^B) : q=0,1,…,2^B−1},  n=1,…,N.   (1)
For a fixed configuration X=(W,Θ), the SINR of user k in cell l is
γ_lk(X,h) = |h_lk^H w_lk|² / (Σ_(i,j)≠(l,k) |h_lk^H w_ij|² + σ²).   (2)
For each user, the effective channel used by the uncertainty model is the ordered equivalent vector ĥ_lk (the aggregate model stacks the L BS-to-user channel blocks). Its imperfect estimate is modeled as
h_lk = ĥ_lk + Δh_lk,    ||Δh_lk||₂ ≤ r_lk(ε),    r_lk(ε)=ε·max(||ĥ_lk||₂,r_min).   (3)
For target SINR γ̄_lk, robust QoS requires
γ_lk(X, ĥ_lk+Δh_lk) ≥ γ̄_lk,  for all ||Δh_lk||₂≤r_lk(ε).   (4)
The bandwidth-normalized wireless energy efficiency used in this paper is
WEE(X) = [Σ_l,k log₂(1+γ_lk(X))] / P_tot(X),   (5)
where P_tot includes transmit, circuit, and RIS hardware/controller power. Since system bandwidth is common to all candidates, WEE is reported in bit/s/Hz/W; multiplying the numerator by a common bandwidth would not change candidate ordering or selection.
The robust WEE design problem is
max_{W,Θ} WEE(W,Θ)
s.t. γ_lk(W,Θ,Δh) ≥ γ̄_lk,
     ∀l,k, ∀Δh: ||Δh||₂≤r_lk(ε),
     W∈𝒲, Θ∈𝒬_B^N.   (6)
The present paper treats X as fixed during certification. Candidate configurations can be generated by the preceding robust design stage or by any other feasible configuration-generation method. In the numerical protocol, B=2 is fixed and the pool is generated on one shared nominal channel using discrete phase mixtures with alignment fraction/jitter, a centralized ZF initialization that also suppresses inter-cell leakage, worst-case fixed-point power control at several design uncertainty levels and SINR margins, and optional feasible power slack/perturbation. The certification layer does not re-optimize X; its purpose is to quantify and compare the robustness of obtained configurations.
3. Statewise Robust QoS Reformulation
For fixed X, each robust QoS constraint can be written as a quadratic inequality
(ĥ+Δh)^H A_lk(X)(ĥ+Δh) − γ̄_lk σ² ≥ 0.   (7)
The uncertainty set is
Δh^H I Δh − r_lk(ε)^2 ≤ 0.   (8)
By the S-procedure, the robust QoS condition is equivalent, under the standard regularity condition, to the existence of λ_lk≥0 such that
M_lk(X,ε,λ_lk) =
[ A_lk+λ_lk I,              A_lk ĥ_lk
  ĥ_lk^H A_lk,  ĥ_lk^H A_lk ĥ_lk−γ̄_lk σ²−λ_lk r_lk(ε)^2 ] ⪰ 0.   (9)
Thus, for fixed X and ε, robust feasibility can be checked by finding nonnegative multipliers satisfying (9) for every user. This statewise feasibility test is the oracle used by the proposed certificate computation. The equivalence concerns fixed-state robust QoS feasibility and does not imply global optimality of the overall mixed discrete-continuous WEE problem.
4. Configuration-Specific Robustness Certificate
For a fixed configuration X that is nominally feasible, define the robustness-feasibility indicator
F_X(ε) = 1 if X satisfies all robust QoS constraints for relative uncertainty factor ε, and F_X(ε) = 0 otherwise.   (10)
ε_cert(X) = sup { ε ≥ 0 : F_X(ε)=1 }.   (11)
ε_cert(X) is the largest dimensionless relative uncertainty factor that the already-fixed configuration can tolerate while preserving robust QoS under the adopted relative-radius model. Because r_lk(ε) grows monotonically with ε, the uncertainty sets are nested: if X is feasible at ε₂, it is also feasible at every 0 ≤ ε₁ ≤ ε₂. Hence F_X is monotone non-increasing and ε_cert is the boundary between the certified and uncertified factor regions. The implementation returns a conservative lower endpoint of the final bisection bracket.
Because F_X(ε) is monotone, the certificate can be obtained by bisection. With bounds ε_low and ε_high,
 ε_mid=(ε_low+ε_high)/2.   (12)
If F_X(ε_mid)=1, set ε_low=ε_mid; otherwise set ε_high=ε_mid. Stop when
 ε_high−ε_low ≤ δ_ε.   (13)
The returned ε_low is a conservative numerical certificate. No beamforming or RIS re-optimization is required during this stage. For numerical robustness, an implementation may also distinguish an exact bisection bracket from a lower-bound-censored result when the search cap remains feasible.
5. Certified Reuse Horizon (Corollary)
Let the reference relative channel state be ĥ(0) and let the normalized channel deviation be bounded by a general nondecreasing function ρ(t), expressed in the same relative-uncertainty units as ε_cert:
 ||ĥ(t)−ĥ(0)||₂ / max(||ĥ(0)||₂,r_min) ≤ ρ(t).   (14)
A fixed configuration X is certified as reusable at time t whenever
 ρ(t) ≤ ε_cert(X).
Thus define the certified reuse horizon as
T_cert(X)=sup{t ≥ 0 : ρ(t) ≤ ε_cert(X)}.   (15)
This definition does not require linear channel evolution. As a special case, if the adopted deterministic drift model is ρ(t)=νt with ν>0, then
 T_cert(X)=ε_cert(X)/ν.   (16)
Hence the division by ν is a direct consequence of a particular drift model, not an independent optimization result. T_cert is a conservative certificate under the assumed drift model, not a prediction of the actual QoS failure time.
6. Robustness-Aware Configuration Selection and Dominance Filtering
However, a candidate with lower WEE can have a substantially larger robustness certificate. We therefore consider the two-dimensional representation (WEE(X), ε_cert(X)). A configuration Xi dominates Xj if
 WEE(Xi) ≥ WEE(Xj)  and  ε_cert(Xi) ≥ ε_cert(Xj),
with at least one strict inequality. Dominated configurations can be removed before threshold-based selection because they cannot be preferred by a policy that is nondecreasing in both WEE and certified robustness. The remaining nondominated set forms the WEE-robustness Pareto frontier.
To account for configuration stability, we then impose a minimum robustness requirement ε_min and solve
 X_sel = arg max_{X∈𝒳} WEE(X)
 s.t. ε_cert(X) ≥ ε_min.   (18)
Under the linear drift model in Section 5, the same rule may equivalently be expressed using a minimum certified reuse horizon,
 ε_cert(X) ≥ νT_min,   equivalently   T_cert(X) ≥ T_min.   (19)
Thus, configurations that cannot satisfy the required robustness or reuse duration are first filtered out; among the remaining nondominated candidates, the configuration with the highest WEE is selected. In the implementation, Pareto filtering is performed explicitly before the threshold. The robustness-only rule X_R=arg max ε_cert(X) can be reported as a reference, but it is not the proposed objective.
7. Proposed Certification and Selection Framework
The framework contains five stages: (i) generate a pool of nominally QoS-feasible candidate beamforming/RIS configurations; (ii) certify each fixed configuration by bisection using the statewise LMI feasibility test; (iii) construct the WEE-ε_cert candidate map and remove dominated configurations; (iv) apply a minimum-robustness requirement and select the highest-WEE remaining nondominated candidate; and (v) when a drift model is available, map the selected certificate to a conservative reuse horizon.
The complete logic is
 Candidate Pool → Statewise Robust Feasibility → ε_cert → Dominance/Pareto Filtering → Stability-Aware Selection → [T_cert].   (20)
The certification layer is modular and can be applied to any candidate for which the statewise robust QoS feasibility test is available. Candidate generation is decoupled from certification, while the selection layer uses only candidate-level WEE and ε_cert values. This separation allows the proposed framework to be attached to different robust RIS optimizers without changing the underlying candidate-generation algorithm.
8. Complexity Analysis
Let C_LMI be the cost of one statewise feasibility check and I_bis the number of bisection iterations. Certification of one configuration costs approximately
O(I_bis C_LMI).   (21)
For an initial interval [ε_low,ε_high] and tolerance δ_ε,
I_bis = O(log₂((ε_high−ε_low)/δ_ε)).   (22)
For J candidates, the certification overhead is
O(J I_bis C_LMI).   (23)
Certification does not introduce a new search over the discrete RIS space and does not repeat the full WEE optimization.
9. Numerical Evaluation
The numerical study is organized into three main experiments so that the core claims can be demonstrated within a conference-paper page budget. The same candidate-generation protocol and system parameters are used throughout, and only configurations passing the prescribed QoS feasibility check enter the certification stage.
Experiment 1 — Certificate consistency and empirical validation. Several representative feasible configurations are selected from the same certified pool. First, deterministic LMI checks are evaluated at normalized points αε_cert with α∈{0.90,0.95,0.99,1.00,1.01,1.05,1.10}, verifying the boundary behavior of the bisection certificate. Second, an independent Monte Carlo sweep samples uncertainty directions inside the relative balls over 0–3ε_cert. The sampled violation rate is reported only as empirical validation; it is never used to estimate ε_cert.
Experiment 2 — Efficiency–robustness landscape and Pareto structure. A pool of 40 unique feasible configurations is built on one shared nominal channel realization. The paper fixes B=2 for all candidates. Every candidate is certified by bisection and plotted in the (WEE, ε_cert) plane. The WEE-only choice, robustness-only choice, and nondominated Pareto set are highlighted. The current reference run contains 40 unique configurations and 2 nondominated points; the WEE and certificate values are not artificially modified to force a tradeoff. This experiment tests whether current efficiency and certified robustness contain complementary configuration information.
Experiment 3 — Stability-aware selection and requirement sensitivity. Three selection rules are compared: WEE-only, robustness-only, and the proposed Pareto-first robustness-constrained WEE rule. The minimum robustness requirement is swept over {0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, 0.95}ε_cert,max. The current reference run reports the selected WEE, ε_cert, conditional T_cert, and the number of nondominated candidates remaining after threshold filtering. An independent-sample check evaluates QoS at the prescribed design factor ε=0.05 for the selected configurations. An additional multi-seed generalization check is intended as a compact holdout analysis rather than a fourth main experiment.
Across all experiments, the prescribed design uncertainty factor ε=0.05 and the post-optimization certificate ε_cert must be reported separately. The design factor is an input requirement used during candidate generation or system design, whereas ε_cert measures a property of a fixed candidate after generation. T_cert additionally depends on the channel-drift assumption. For the current reference run, the generated pool contains 40 accepted configurations after 125 attempts; 10 candidates have ε_cert<0.05. The WEE range is [0.6319,1.2792], the ε_cert range is [0.00095,0.11396], and the Spearman correlation is ρ=0.856 (p≈1.95×10^-12). The WEE-only choice is candidate 34 (WEE=1.2792, ε_cert=0.09089), while the robustness-only choice is candidate 6 (WEE=1.2460, ε_cert=0.11396). At the 0.90ε_cert,max requirement, the stability-aware rule selects candidate 6. In the sensitivity sweep, the selection switches from candidate 34 to candidate 6 between 0.75ε_cert,max and 0.90ε_cert,max. At ε=0.05, the three reported selections achieve 100% QoS hold rate over the implemented 500-sample independent uncertainty check. These values are descriptive for the stated reference seed and protocol, not estimates of universal performance.
10. Discussion
The proposed certificate provides configuration-level information that a conventional robust design result does not directly report. Two feasible configurations can have different certified relative uncertainty factors even when their current WEE values are similar. The WEE-robustness map then reveals whether one configuration dominates another and identifies the nondominated set that contains the meaningful candidates for stability-aware selection. In the current reference run, only two candidates are nondominated, so the empirical Pareto frontier should be interpreted as a property of the generated candidate pool rather than as a dense approximation of the globally attainable frontier.
The interpretation is model-dependent. ε_cert is defined with respect to the adopted relative uncertainty set and QoS constraints. T_cert additionally depends on the assumed drift-rate or channel-aging model and is therefore a derived temporal interpretation rather than a standalone robustness metric. Statistical channel-aging models or empirically calibrated drift bounds can lead to different lifetime interpretations. The reported lifetime should be described as a certified reuse horizon under explicit assumptions, not as the actual QoS failure time.
The framework also does not claim global optimality for the underlying discrete RIS WEE optimization. Its contribution is a configuration-level certification, dominance filtering, and selection layer after candidate configurations have been obtained. The practical benefit therefore depends on the quality and diversity of the candidate pool; certification does not create new candidates by itself. The current candidate-generation protocol is deliberately lightweight and uses a fixed centralized-ZF direction family, so the observed landscape is not intended to characterize every possible feasible configuration.
11. Conclusion
This paper proposed a configuration-specific robustness certification framework for discrete RIS-assisted multi-cell networks. For a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration, the framework determines the maximum relative CSI-uncertainty factor preserving robust QoS through a statewise S-procedure-based feasibility test and bisection. The resulting certificate defines a configuration-level robustness axis on which feasible candidates can be dominance-filtered and selected jointly with WEE. A reuse horizon is obtained only as a conditional corollary under an explicit relative channel-drift model. The framework therefore extends static robust RIS optimization with a reusable certification and configuration-management layer, without introducing another full discrete optimization procedure.
Key notation: X=(W,Θ) denotes a fixed beamforming/RIS configuration; F_X(ε) is its robust-feasibility indicator; ε_cert is its configuration-specific certified relative uncertainty factor; T_cert is the conditional certified reuse horizon; ε_min is the minimum robustness requirement; ε=0.05 is the prescribed design uncertainty factor; ν is the adopted linear relative channel-drift-rate bound.
